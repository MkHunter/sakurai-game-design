## C01 — Frame Rates
*C: Planning & Game Design · 04:00 · [watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Rjdmi7628GM)*

**Technique:** Treating Frames as Discrete Temporal Units — In video games, frames function as discrete units of time rather than continuous flow. Because spatial displacement relies on the formula Distance = Speed × Time, the frame rate dictates how frequently physics, velocities, and input queries update per second.
- ✅ Structure game logic, collision detection, and animation states using discrete frame counts as your baseline temporal measurement.
- ✅ Ensure movement math accounts for discrete frame intervals so character displacement remains consistent across varying update frequencies.
- ✅ Target 60 fps for action games requiring tight control responsiveness, as doubling update frequency halves input latency and visual displacement steps.
- ❌ Treating game time as a purely abstract floating-point second without accounting for the discrete refresh slices of display hardware.
- ❌ Allowing animation and physics to step at unpredictable intervals without clamping or fixed-tick simulation.
**Technique:** Decoupling Game Simulation from Display Refresh — Tying game simulation updates directly to hardware vertical refresh interrupts causes simulation speed to fluctuate across hardware standards with different refresh rates. Modern architectures should decouple internal game logic time from display rendering cycles to preserve intended gameplay tempo 
- ✅ Decouple internal gameplay simulation ticks from visual frame rendering interrupts.
- ✅ Implement variable-frame-rate rendering or delta-time simulation to maintain real-world physics speed when rendering dips occur, rather than letting the entire game enter physical slowdown.
- ✅ Ensure visual display settings (refresh rate, V-Sync) do not distort the underlying logic tick rate.
- ❌ Binding gameplay logic updates directly to screen vertical blank interrupts (VBLANK), which historically caused PAL ports to run at 5/6th (50 Hz vs 60 Hz) of their intended game speed.
- ❌ Confusing visual frame dropping (skipping redrawing) with simulation slowdown (loss of gameplay execution speed).
> In the Planning & Game Design category, I'll go over what you need to know when coming up with game ideas and mechanics.
>
> I suspect most avid gamers already know this, but I'll explain anyway since it's crucial.
>
> In the game industry, "frame rate" measures how often the screen is redrawn.
>
> Think of it like the frames of a film projector.
>
> On standard televisions, the screen is redrawn or "refreshed" 60 times each second—that's 60 frames per second (fps).

## D01 — Stop for Big Moments!
*D: Design Specifics · 04:19 · [watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OdVkEOzdCPw)*

**Technique:** Hit Stop for Attack Weight — Freezing attacker and defender animations for a brief duration upon collision creates tactile weight and physical resistance. Without this freeze, even massive attacks feel light, floaty, and lacking in kinetic force.
- ✅ Freeze the character animations and impact visual effects for a few frames immediately upon collision contact.
- ✅ Scale the duration of the freeze frame proportionally to the intended weight and power of the attack.
- ❌ Allowing attack animations and hit reactions to continue playing continuously with zero freeze, causing hits to feel hollow and unimpactful.
**Technique:** Boss Stop and Death Pause — Hold the entire screen in a prolonged freeze when a major enemy or player is defeated before playing the destruction explosion or transition. This allows the player to fully absorb their moment of victory or defeat rather than rushing past it.
- ✅ Trigger a full-screen pause combined with a visual cue (like a screen flash) the exact instant a boss's HP drops to zero.
- ✅ Halt the screen immediately upon player death so the player has time to process the fatal collision before transitioning.
- ❌ Instantly exploding or despawning a boss without pausing, robbing the player of the dramatic culmination of the battle.
**Technique:** Impact Freeze for Death Readability — Pause the action at the exact point of impact when a player dies, particularly in fast-paced genres like shoot 'em ups. Freezing the screen on the collision point ensures the player clearly understands what killed them, preventing unfair disorientation.
- ✅ Freeze the screen for a moment on the exact frame of fatal collision before triggering destruction animations.
- ✅ Ensure the visual point of impact is unobscured so the player immediately registers their error.
- ❌ Instantly blowing up the player ship mid-motion, leaving the player wondering 'Wait, I got hit?'
**Technique:** Layered Feedback Hierarchy for Big Moments — Enhance major impact moments by progressively stacking distinct audiovisual effects: starting with a hard stop, adding screen shake, and finishing with slow-motion decay. Layering these techniques amplifies satisfaction without requiring expensive assets.
- ✅ Layer feedback sequentially: begin with a sudden freeze (hit stop), add camera shake to communicate raw force, and trail off with slow motion.
- ✅ Scale the intensity and number of stacked layers according to the significance of the event (e.g., standard enemy vs large battleship).
- ❌ Relying solely on destruction particle effects without stopping screen or motion, resulting in an underwhelming, frictionless explosion.
> I consider this topic to be quite an important one.

>

## I09 — Strong Attack, Light Reverb
*I: Audio · 03:00 · [watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-nWjT49-cds)*

> For one, the impact sound should never feel like it's coming too late.
>
> In order to sound right, it can't simply start at peak volume, but it's important that we get to a strong sound almost immediately.
>
> Next, don't drag out the reverberation too much.
>
> In particular, lighter sound effects for attacks like rapid jabs that hit repeatedly need to be pared down to just long enough to still feel like consecutive hits.
>
> The sound keeps playing in the meantime, but ideally the main part of the sound effect should be over before the action resumes.
