# Sakurai: direction, teamwork & marketing

Making games is a job, not a game (A01). It runs on fast decisions, open information and respect
across the team, and once it ships it still needs people to find out about it.

## When to use

- Defining team workflows: reviews, decisions, communication, QA, playtesting, tooling.
- Advising a director or lead on a contested decision or a feedback culture.
- Planning trailers, demos, websites or live demos.

**Not for:** writing the proposal or spec → `sakurai-planning-and-specs`.

## Directing

- **The director decides. The majority shouldn't always rule.** Votes trend toward the most
  mediocre option. The person holding the big-picture vision should set direction (A15, E13).
  If roles are split, be extremely clear about who has the final say.
- **Don't put decisions off.** Decide on the spot, and have the team help make decisions
  possible. Stay flexible enough to correct mistakes later. "Later is too late" (E10).
- **Finish everything within the day.** Respond to every review request the same day, first
  come first served. Delays stall whole sub-teams (E09).
- **A director's job is to trim.** Choose the best short- and long-term solution as limits,
  deadlines and workload shift. Pass the baton quickly and always give accurate information
  (E15).
- **Directors need to be unique,** with balance and the ability to communicate their ideas (A08).
- **Remember the player.** "Try telling that to the player." Balance the ideal against what's
  technically feasible (A03).
- **"You'll forget the hardship, but the game will endure."** Avoid regrettable compromises,
  without imposing needless hardship (A07).
- **Target with a top-down view of the market,** not your own tastes (A12). Take market research
  with a grain of salt when blazing new paths, because following it leads to average work (L08).
- **Broad input, broad output.** Play many games through to the ending, consume media actively,
  and produce regularly (A13).

## Team communication

- **Share information.** Have sub-teams present their progress so nobody feels like a cog, and
  so people present work made for others (E01, E04).
- **Explain ideas to everyone at once.** Hold big open review sessions with a mic and a screen,
  open to anyone. Keep them speedy (E05).
- **Daily reports** with varied content keep the bigger picture visible (E06).
- **Don't wait to speak your mind.** Raise issues immediately to the people who can fix them,
  ideally with a proposed solution (E03, A04).
- **Up is down and down is up.** No one is superior or subservient. Address team members
  respectfully (A23). **Never get into fights or speak ill of people** (A22).
- **Beware echo chambers.** Listen to concerns but keep a distinctive vision (A20).
- **Bigger teams lose efficiency** ("10 people produce 7 people's work"), so invest in tools,
  rules and security (E04).

## Pipeline & tools

- **Name files logically.** Concise names anyone understands at a glance. No romanized Japanese
  on international teams (K01).
- **Tickets** make tasks visible. Avoid chaining tasks so the next person downstream is blocked
  (K04). Bug tickets go to whoever can fix them, whether artist, animator or programmer (E11).
- **Merging shared files** reduces lock contention but still causes conflicts, so plan for them
  (K07).
- **Debug mode** on the same controller as gameplay, kept organized and pruned. Add
  **transparency/visualization features** for collision and the like (K08, K09).

## Testing

- **Bug testing never ends.** Combinations explode, so target risky areas first, but accept the
  limits (E07).
- **Bug-testing system:** everyone on the latest daily build, structured tests against design
  docs, sub-teams per area, and tickets routed to the right discipline (E11).
- **Play testing keeps going.** Skilled, persistent testers inform parameter calls, and split
  opinions among players are healthy (E12). **Elementary-school testers** give honest reactions
  (E14). Tune for newcomers (see `sakurai-game-essence`, B29).
- Platform **guidelines** must be followed, so plan for them (M19). **Mastering up** is the
  final gate (M12).

## Marketing (L01–L09)

- **Marketing is multiplicative.** A great game nobody knows about sells nothing. Make game
  assets reusable for promotion, and review all official communications (L01).
- **Paint an accurate picture.** Title, art and trailers must reflect the real fun. No bait and
  switch (L02).
- **Show the actual game!** Get to gameplay fast in trailers, especially for new IP or indie
  games (L06).
- **Demos** are effective but cost almost as much testing as a release. Strip spoiler data
  (L03).
- **Fit the website** to the game and the era (social media first) (L04). **Reveal videos** need
  shot-by-shot plans including music cues (L05).
- **Playing in front of people:** show every special, keep matches close, and know the content
  (L07). Delegate daily publicity tasks if you're the busy director (L09).

## Review procedure (for a team process)

1. How long does a decision or review request wait? Target the same day.
2. Who has final say on design? Is it explicit?
3. How does information flow? Are there all-hands reviews or presentations? Can anyone raise
   issues early?
4. Check the tooling: file naming, tickets routed by discipline, external parameters, debug
   mode, visualization.
5. Check QA and playtesting: daily builds, structured coverage, newcomer testers.
6. Check marketing: does every external asset show real gameplay accurately?
