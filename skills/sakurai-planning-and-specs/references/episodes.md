## A04 — Spec Changes
*A: Work Ethic · 04:15 · [watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bzWibA7izF8)*

> Changing a game's spec in the middle of development- it's something to avoid whenever you can, but you also need to be flexible.
>
> That'll cost you money, and it might demotivate your team, so you really should try to avoid it.
>
> That is to say, if you want to make your game ready to show to the world, first you have to finish it-- and if you try to envision a finished game right from the get-go, you might spend as much time writing documentation as you do actually developing the game itself.
>
> What you really want to avoid is having g to change course because your game's not fun.
>
> Try to have the fun in mind straight from the planning stages.

## C18 — Avoiding Comparison
*C: Planning & Game Design · 03:05 · [watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gYuvggptkDM)*

> Whenever an innovative new game is announced, whether it's mine or anyone else's, you'll always hear people comparing it to an existing game that they're already familiar with.
>
> It's not easy describing something people have never seen or heard of before, and without a point of reference, they may not understand what you're saying.
>
> But you yourself shouldn't invite the correlation.
>
> First, if your game is truly that analogous to another title, people might just want to play the original instead.
>
> The only games that should point to other games as their selling points are sequels in the same series.
