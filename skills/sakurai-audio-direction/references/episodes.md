## I02 — Strike a Balance with Sound Effects
*I: Audio · 04:48 · [watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qKjD5qbLUeI)*

> In most cases, you'll be choosing between realistic or exaggerated sounds, but you'll still need to think carefully about how loud, how flashy, and how discernible each one is.
>
> Both seem pretty quiet, so which should you prioritize?
>
> My advice is as follows: The more relevant a sound is to the player, the louder that sound should be.
>
> The sound effect that should be loudest is the KO sound.
>
> Whether you're KO'ing an opponent or getting KO'd yourself, it's crucial to the push and pull and the outcome of the match, so it's the loudest effect- top priority.

## I03 — Listen in Various Environments
*I: Audio · 02:43 · [watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WUy4lTUhj80)*

> When running sound checks, I always make sure to listen and compare in multiple environments.
>
> It's important to use a studio with great soundproofing and speaker systems to get the sound just right, but if you focus too much on what you can hear in that pristine environment, you risk your details being inaudible on most people's everyday speakers.
>
> But it's crucial to think about the environments your players will be listening in.
>
> More than anything, I recommend not solely using big studio speakers to finalize sound.
>
> Some might say that mimicking the audio from the original game would be enough, but we also wanted to capture how it sounded coming from the arcade cabinet- a process that required lots of careful trial and error.

## I09 — Strong Attack, Light Reverb
*I: Audio · 03:00 · [watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-nWjT49-cds)*

> For one, the impact sound should never feel like it's coming too late.
>
> In order to sound right, it can't simply start at peak volume, but it's important that we get to a strong sound almost immediately.
>
> Next, don't drag out the reverberation too much.
>
> In particular, lighter sound effects for attacks like rapid jabs that hit repeatedly need to be pared down to just long enough to still feel like consecutive hits.
>
> The sound keeps playing in the meantime, but ideally the main part of the sound effect should be over before the action resumes.

## I11 — Balancing Ambient Sounds
*I: Audio · 02:01 · [watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jAgi6Uk1CU0)*

> As I've stated before, ambient sounds are the least important and take lowest priority.
>
> With games, it's important to make players feel like they've really entered another world.
>
> I'd say that sense of immersion is especially key when using first-person or over-the-shoulder viewpoints.
>
> As opposed to other sound effects which generally need to be more exaggerated, relatively realistic audio works great for ambient sounds.
>
> Take care in setting up ambient sounds, and do your best to make players feel as immersed as possible.
