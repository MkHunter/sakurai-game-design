## H01 — Make It "Pop"
*H: Effects · 02:32 · [watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kcYDrtRvuKg)*

**Technique:** Hard-Edged Shape Layering in VFX — Relying purely on soft additive particle blending results in hazy, washed-out effects that lack punch and definition. By compositing crisp, flat-shaded polygonal shards, sharp sprites, or stylized geometric shapes over particle glow layers, the effect gains a defined focal point and silhouette.
- ✅ Overlay distinct, hard-edged polygonal meshes or sharp 2D sprites directly into the core of soft particle bursts.
- ✅ Use stylistically clean, opaque geometric elements to serve as the visual focal point rather than letting diffuse glow dominate.
- ❌ Letting additive blending do all the work with purely soft, glowing particles that blur together without a discernible core silhouette.
**Technique:** Value Contrast Injection for VFX — Purely luminous visual effects disappear when displayed over bright or daytime environments because they lack local value contrast. Injecting dark graphic elements, dark smoke, or internal silhouettes into bright flashes ensures the effect retains punch regardless of background luminance.
- ✅ Embed dark, high-contrast shapes (such as black/brown outline rings or dark smoke cores) inside bright lightning and fire effects.
- ✅ Test every visual effect across both extreme light (daytime sky) and extreme dark (space/night) stages to verify value separation.
- ❌ Designing VFX exclusively against black test viewports, which conceals whether additive effects blow out on sunny or light backgrounds.
- ❌ Attempting to fix unreadable bright effects by forcing entire game environments to remain dark.
> Don't let additive blending do all the work for you!
>
> I'd recommend mixing in crisp effects where you can.
>
> With any kind of design—not just effects—it's best to think about how opposing elements might help your imagery stand out.

## F01 — Draw the Light, Not the Asset
*F: Graphics · 03:32 · [watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FuAtKjEuck8)*

**Technique:** Light-Centric Surface Asset Styling — Assets should not be finalized simply with their local base colors (such as brown for a trunk and green for leaves); they must be designed around how light bounces, scatters, and reaches the eye. Simulating light reflection, hue shifts, and atmospheric interactions on surfaces creates visual depth a
- ✅ Never consider an asset finished based solely on local albedo colors; incorporate the behavior of the environmental light it will reflect.
- ✅ Introduce subtle variations in hue across surfaces to simulate the natural scattering of light.
- ✅ Apply gradient sprays to accentuate surfaces facing primary light sources to generate contrast across asset groupings.
- ✅ Incorporate atmospheric perspective—such as directional brightness filters or atmospheric haze—to establish depth and distance.
- ❌ Drawing an isolated asset with flat, uniform material colors (e.g., pure brown trunk, pure green leaves) and considering it complete.
- ❌ Relying entirely on complex runtime shaders or post-processing passes to fix flat, lifeless base textures.
**Technique:** Simpler Techniques for Asset Density Optimization — To maximize the number of assets rendered concurrently, artists should use simple composite lighting adjustments rather than heavy per-object shaders or dense physical geometry. Reducing the per-object processing burden directly allows hardware to support far more elements on screen.
- ✅ Prioritize lightweight 2D/3D composite adjustments (such as color sprays, sunlight brightness filters, and hue shifts) over costly per-model real-time shaders.
- ✅ Reduce per-asset polygon and shader complexity to free hardware headroom for higher on-screen object counts.
- ✅ Disguise asset duplication by layering light gradients and atmospheric filters over grouped instances instead of generating unique heavy models.
- ❌ Modeling excessive physical geometry (such as individual leaves on a tree) that overburdens hardware and restricts the scene's asset density.
- ❌ Assuming powerful hardware will compensate for unoptimized rendering through expensive brute-force post-processing.
> So I'll try to keep these brief.
>
> These issues come up often in game dev, and they're important to consider for both current and aspiring artists, so I'll try to cover them clearly.
>
> When artists show me their finished work, particularly background artists, I often have the same comment: Don't draw the asset, draw the light it reflects.
>
> With a powerful enough machine, we might be able to add detail through shaders or other post-processing, but we don't often have that luxury.
>
> Which means it's better to go for simpler techniques when possible.
